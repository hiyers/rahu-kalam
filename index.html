    What to avoid and what is allowed
    • What to avoid: Starting new ventures, signing important contracts, making major purchases, or performing auspicious ceremonies (like weddings or housewarmings).
    • What is allowed: Routine daily tasks, ongoing projects, and regular work can continue as usual without interruption.
    Buffer and rounding

    The big time adds your buffer to both sides, then rounds the start down and the end up so it is easy to remember. The exact time is shown under it. Duration is the length of the big time including the buffer, and each of the 8 parts is the exact length of Rahu Kalam.

      About

      The time between sunrise and sunset is split into 8 equal parts. Rahu Kalam is one of them, chosen by the weekday: Monday 2nd, Tuesday 7th, Wednesday 5th, Thursday 6th, Friday 4th, Saturday 3rd, Sunday 8th, so it lasts as long as one block. Drag along the bar to check any time of day: it says Caution inside Rahu Kalam and your buffer, and Good otherwise.

      Sunrise and sunset come from a standard solar formula and are usually within a couple of minutes. Panchangs differ in how they define sunrise and in rounding, so for rituals check your local almanac. Rahu Kalam is a tradition from Hindu astrology, where the period is generally avoided for starting new ventures.

      The times are worked out inside this page. When you search, the text you type is sent to the free Open-Meteo place search to find matching cities, and nothing else is sent. Your last choice is saved in this browser on this device, and nowhere else.

      Sources. The weekday rule comes from Wikipedia and Drik Panchang. Date and time come from your device clock and are shown in the selected city's time zone.
